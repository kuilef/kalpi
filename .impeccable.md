## Design Context

### Users

Russian-speaking Israeli voters use Kalpi to articulate policy preferences and compare them with party positions. Researchers and careful voters use the separate analytics view to audit evidence quality rather than accepting a black-box recommendation.

### Brand Personality

Restrained, editorial, and candid. The interface should feel like a serious magazine questionnaire: calm enough for reflection, precise about uncertainty, and never like a campaign dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use the existing warm-paper, ink, and rust palette with serif editorial headlines and compact sans-serif metadata. Prefer tables, rules, disclosure, and clear reading order over decorative cards, gradients, or ideological maps.

### Design Principles

- Keep the questionnaire as the first task; show results only after completion.
- Make provenance and uncertainty legible without obscuring the primary answer.
- Use colour as a secondary cue; labels, numbers, and accessible controls carry the meaning.
- Preserve comfortable reading and usable tables at 390px.
- State political limitations plainly: proximity is not voting advice.
